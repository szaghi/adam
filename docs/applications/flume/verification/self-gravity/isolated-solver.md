## Case and scope

On a periodic domain a self-gravitating object feels its own images, and the mean density must be removed. Isolated
boundaries give the free-space potential, $\Phi \to 0$ at infinity, of the mass on the grid: what a star, a collapsing
cloud or a binary needs. P4 adds them to the FFT solver and verifies the solver (SV-6, this page), a star in
equilibrium (SV-7) and a collapsing one (SV-8, both on [the n = 1 polytrope](./polytrope)).

**The method** (`poisson_bc = isolated`). Hockney and Eastwood's zero padding: the density sits in an array of
$m = 2(N + n_{gc})$ cells per direction, zero outside the domain, and is convolved, by one r2c transform, a product and
one c2r, with the **lattice** Green's function of $L_h$, $h^2G(\mathbf n)$. The circular convolution equals the
free-space one on every cell the scatter reads, ghosts included, because $m$ exceeds twice the largest offset. Using
the lattice kernel of $L_h$ rather than the sampled continuum $-1/(4\pi r)$ makes $\Phi$ satisfy the discrete Poisson
equation, not just approximate it.

**The kernel.** $G$ is generated once, offline, by `scripts/lattice_green.py` into `adam_lattice_green_library` (6545
values, the wedge $n_1 \ge n_2 \ge n_3 \ge 0$):
- **near field**, $\max|n_d| \le 32$: the Dirichlet problem $L_1G = \delta$ on the cube $[-32, 32]^3$, solved exactly
  by a type-I sine transform, with the far-field series on its faces;
- **far field**: the series $G_0 + G_1 + G_2 + G_3$, $G_0 = -1/(4\pi r)$,
  $G_1 = -5(n_1^4 + n_2^4 + n_3^4 - \tfrac35r^4)/(32\pi r^7)$, $G_2 = P_8/r^{13}$, $G_3 = P_{12}/r^{19}$, each term
  solving its order of the Taylor series of $L_1$ (checked symbolically). The series leaves one coefficient free in
  $G_2$ and one in $G_3$ (the cubic harmonics of degree 4 and 6); the lattice fixes them, and they are fitted to the
  table (least squares over $12 \le r \le 26$).

What the case catches:
- a kernel that is not the lattice Green's function of $L_h$ (a sampled continuum kernel, a bad table/series seam):
  the residual of $L_h$ leaves the kernel's level;
- a padding too small for the offsets the scatter reads: the error against the exact potential on the ghosts and the
  padded far field;
- a wrong mass normalisation or sign: the error against the exact potentials outside the source ($-GM/r$);
- a solver that accepts a configuration it cannot solve: the scope is fatal otherwise (3-D, every direction
  non-periodic, cubic cells: the kernel is the unit lattice's).

## Initial and boundary conditions

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Domain | the cube $[-1, 1]^3$, non-periodic |
| Grid | octree levels 1–3 ($16^3$ to $64^3$ cells), 8 cells per block side, 3 ghosts |
| Ranks | CPU: 1, 2, 3; FNL: 1, 2 |
| Factor | $4\pi G$ with $G = 1$ |
| Source 1: the n = 1 polytrope | $\rho = \sin(kr)/(kr)$, $k = \pi/a$, $a = 1/2$ (smooth; its derivative jumps at $r = a$); exact $\Phi = -(4\pi/k^2)\sin(kr)/(kr) - M/a$ inside, $-M/r$ outside, $M = 4a^3/\pi$ |
| Source 2: the uniform sphere | $\rho = 1$, $r < a$, as cell averages ($8^3$ samples per cell); exact $-2\pi(a^2 - r^2/3)$ inside, $-M/r$ outside, $M = 4\pi a^3/3$ |
| Refusals | `--refuse-isolated-periodic` (a periodic grid), `--refuse-isolated-anisotropic` (cells $1/8\times2/8\times3/8$), `--refuse-isolated-2d` (null $z$): each must stop with exit 1 and its message |

## Findings

- **The kernel series needs $G_2$ and $G_3$.** With $G_0 + G_1$ alone the composite residual was $7\cdot10^{-10}$,
  concentrated on the cube's faces, and summed over a $64^3$ source it showed as a residual of $3.4\cdot10^{-10}$ in
  $\Phi$; with $G_2$ and $G_3$ they are $1.0\cdot10^{-13}$ and $2.1\cdot10^{-11}$.
- **The residual is the kernel's, not round-off.** It is $10^{-14}$ while every offset lies inside the table ($16^3$,
  $32^3$) and $2\cdot10^{-11}$ at $64^3$, where the offsets reach the series: about $10^{-13}$ per offset, summed over
  the source cells. At $128^3$ (measured once, 6 ranks) it is $5\cdot10^{-11}$.
- **The polytrope converges at second order** (2.14, 1.92, 2.02 per pair from $16^3$ to $128^3$; the dip is the kink
  of its density at the surface). **The uniform sphere converges irregularly** (0.6, 2.1, 1.75): its surface cuts
  cells, and the bound only asserts better than first order.
- **Conservation needs symmetry, not a round-off residual.** The convolution with an even kernel is a symmetric linear
  map, which is what the exact exchange of $E$ and $W$ needs ([models](../../models#self-gravity)):
  [SV-8](./polytrope) measures it.
- The error is largest at the centre, where the potential is deepest.
